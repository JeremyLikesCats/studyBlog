# Introduction, Vector Functions and Electrostatics

## Vector Functions
Vector functions are simply functions that can be expressed like this:
$$
\mathbf{F}(x, y, z) = F_x(x, y, z) \hat{i} + F_y(x, y, z) \hat{j} + F_z(x, y, z) \hat{k}.
$$
They specify a *magnitude* and *direction* for every point $(x, y, z)$.


## Electrostatics

A fundamental property of nature is "charge". There are two kinds of charge - positive/negative. 

**Coulomb's law** states that the force between two charges $q$ and $q_0$ is:
$$
\mathbf{F_q} = \frac{1}{4 \pi \epsilon_0} \frac{qq_0}{r^2} \hat{u}
$$
where $\hat{u}$ points from $q_0$ to $q$.

The **Electric field** is defined by:
$$
\mathbf{E} = \frac{\mathbf{F_q}}{q}.
$$

Another very important property is **superposition**. Superposition states that the presence of other forces does not affect the original force itself, allowing us to simply add forces vectorially.
