# Electricity and Magnetism Chapter 2: Part 1.

## Potential

Electric Potential is defined as:

$$\phi = -\int \mathbf{E} \cdot d\mathbf{s}.$$

It has the units of Joules/Coulomb. Note that it's different to potential **energy** as it uses a field instead of a force.

The **potential difference** is the difference in potential between two points. Often, we fix one of the points at infinity. However, this doesn't always work.

For the electric force, this potential is path-independent. 

## The Gradient

The gradient turns a scalar function into a vector one in the following way:

$$\nabla f(x,y,z) = \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} \hat{\mathbf{x}} + \frac{\partial f}{\partial y} \hat{\mathbf{y}} + \frac{\partial f}{\partial z} \hat{\mathbf{z}}.$$

We know that:

$$d\phi = -\mathbf{E} \cdot d\mathbf{s}.$$

We also know that:

$$d\phi = \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial x} dx \hat{\mathbf{x}} + \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial y} dy \hat{\mathbf{y}} + \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial z} dz \hat{\mathbf{z}}.$$

and that:

$$d\mathbf{s} = dx \hat{\mathbf{x}} + dy \hat{\mathbf{y}} + dz \hat{\mathbf{z}}.$$

Therefore, we can infer that $\boxed{\mathbf{E} = -\nabla \phi}$.
